# Chapter 7: post-brushing water rinsing

**Database:** PubMed
**Date run:** 15 August 2026
**Question:** Does refraining from rinsing with water after brushing reduce
dental caries?

## Query

```
post-brushing rinsing water dental caries randomized trial fluoride retention
```

**4 records**, all screened in full.

| PMID | Study | Outcome | Relevant? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 39245740 | Parakaw 2024, crossover, 10 adults | salivary, plasma and urinary fluoride | Surrogate only |
| 35241051 | Albahrani 2022, RCT, 120 adults, 12 arms | salivary fluoride to 90 min | Surrogate only |
| 27102319 | Nazzal 2016, crossover, 32 children | salivary fluoride to 90 min | Surrogate only |
| 20831358 | Al-Mulla 2010, in-situ model, 20 orthodontic patients | enamel demineralisation by QLF | Confounded, see below |

## Findings

**No randomised trial isolating post-brushing rinsing with a caries outcome was
found.** Every study since 2000 measures salivary fluoride retention, which is
the same surrogate problem as chapter 6.

The three surrogate studies agree with each other and point the right way: not
rinsing leaves more fluoride in saliva, for roughly 30 to 90 minutes. Parakaw
2024 additionally reports no significant difference in systemic fluoride
absorption, which is reassuring on safety.

**Al-Mulla 2010 deserves a note.** It is the closest thing to a caries outcome,
using an in-situ model with orthodontic bands and quantitative light-induced
fluorescence. But the test regimen was 5,000ppm fluoride toothpaste **and** no
rinsing, against a control of 1,450ppm **with** three rinses. Two variables were
changed at once, so the result cannot be attributed to the rinsing. It is the
same confounded-comparison problem the chapter identifies in the Swedish trial,
thirty years later.

Nazzal 2016 concludes that its results "support the current recommendation" of
spitting without rinsing. That conclusion is about salivary fluoride, and the
recommendation is about tooth decay. This is the step at which a surrogate
quietly becomes a clinical claim.

## Conclusion

Thirty years after the Swedish trial, nobody has run the straightforward
randomised comparison of rinsing against not rinsing with a caries outcome. The
literature has moved entirely to the surrogate.
