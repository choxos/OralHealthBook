# Chapter 17: interdental cleaning

**Database:** PubMed
**Date run:** 21 August 2026

## Source

Cochrane review [@worthington2019], CD012018.pub2, search to 16 January 2019.
Checked for a `.pub3` update: none found, current version.

**Full text available.** Unlike most reviews in this book, the complete review
including characteristics of included studies and risk-of-bias tables was
supplied during writing. Claims about blinding, baseline gingival inflammation
and per-trial sample sizes come from the review body.

## Key figures, from the review

35 RCTs, 3,929 randomised adults.

Devices vs toothbrushing alone: floss (15 trials), interdental brushes (2),
wooden sticks (2), rubber/elastomeric sticks (2), oral irrigators (5).
Head to head vs floss: interdental brushes (9), wooden sticks (3), rubber sticks
(9), oral irrigators (2). Rubber sticks vs interdental brushes (3).

Interdental brushes may reduce gingivitis more than floss at 1 and 3 months
(low certainty); no difference for probing pocket depth.

Adverse events: no severe events; no evidence of between-group differences in
minor effects such as gingival irritation.

## THE finding

> "No trials assessed interproximal caries, and most did not assess
> periodontitis."

35 trials, zero measuring the outcome flossing is popularly believed to prevent.

## Limits stated by the review

- All trials at high risk of performance bias; blinding impossible.
- Only 2 of 35 otherwise at low risk of bias.
- "Many participants had a low level of baseline gingival inflammation" -> floor
  effect, which would bias toward the null.
- Outcomes mostly 1 to 6 months.
- "the effect sizes observed may not be clinically important" (authors' words).

## Correction logged

The original blog post said the 2019 review "showed that we still don't have any
trial evaluating the impact of flossing+toothbrushing versus toothbrushing
alone." **Wrong: 15 such trials are included.** The correct claim is that no
trial measured interproximal caries. Corrected in the chapter, with the error
shown rather than silently fixed.
