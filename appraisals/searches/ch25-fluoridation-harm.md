**Database:** PubMed
**Date run:** 21 August 2026

## Sources held

- NTP monograph on fluoride exposure and neurodevelopment and cognition (2024),
  full text.
- Taylor KW and colleagues. Fluoride exposure and children's IQ scores: a
  systematic review and meta-analysis. *JAMA Pediatr* 2025. Full text with
  supplementary material.
- Iheozor-Ejiofor 2024 (chapter 24) for fluorosis.

## What this chapter did not do

**No independent appraisal of the 74 included studies**, no assessment of
exposure measurement, no reconstruction of the pooled analyses. The chapter
relies on the reviews' own stratified analyses and says so. It is the least
independent chapter in the book.

## Figures used, and one correction

From Taylor 2025:

- 74 studies; **52 at high risk of bias, 22 at low**.
- Pooled SMD **−0.45 (95% CI −0.57 to −0.33)**, 59 studies with group-level
  exposure measures.
- Individual-level: **−1.63 IQ points (−2.33 to −0.93)** per 1 mg/L urinary
  fluoride; **−1.14 (−1.68 to −0.61)** among low risk-of-bias studies.
- Water fluoride, all studies, <1.5 mg/L: β **0.05 (−0.36 to 0.45)**, P = .82.
- Water fluoride, **low risk-of-bias** studies, <1.5 mg/L: β **−0.32 (−0.91 to
  0.26)**, P = .28, 3 studies, 879 children.
- Water fluoride, low risk-of-bias studies, <2 mg/L: β **−0.33 (−0.53 to −0.13)**,
  P = .001.

An earlier draft of the chapter quoted the paper's Key Points sentence and cut it
at a semicolon, **before** the clause reading "and among the subset of low
risk-of-bias studies, there were inverse associations when exposed groups were
restricted to less than 4 mg/L, less than 2 mg/L, and less than 1.5 mg/L for
analyses of fluoride measured both in water and in urine." That omitted clause
complicates the conclusion the chapter then drew, and the quotation and the
conclusion have both been corrected.

From the NTP monograph, page ii: the monograph "does not address whether the sole
exposure to fluoride added to drinking water in some countries (i.e.,
fluoridation, at 0.7 mg/L in the United States and Canada) is associated with a
measurable effect on IQ."
