# Chapter 16: powered against manual toothbrushes

::: provenance
**How this record was produced.** The searches below were run on the dates
stated and disclosed in the chapter at the time. This file was written up
afterwards, from that disclosure and from the working notes, when an audit found

**Database:** PubMed
**Date run:** 21 August 2026

## What was and was not searched

I did **not** run an independent search of the primary toothbrush trial
literature. It is large, industry-dominated, and a single-screener pass over it
would produce a set I would not trust. The chapter is written from two
systematic reviews and says so.

Full text and supplementary material held for both:

- Wang P and colleagues, 2020, on powered versus manual brushing.
- Elkerbout TA, Slot DE, Rosema NAM, Van der Weijden GA, 2020, on plaque removal
  after a single brushing exercise.

## Corrections this record exists to note

From Elkerbout, tables 3 and 4: the **overall** differences of means are
**−0.14 (95% CI −0.19 to −0.09)** on the Quigley and Hein index (15 comparisons)
and **−0.10 (−0.14 to −0.06)** on the Rustogi modified Navy index (8
comparisons). The figures of −0.15 (−0.22 to −0.08) and −0.15 (−0.18 to −0.12)
that appeared in an earlier draft are the **Procter and Gamble** and **Colgate**
brand subanalyses respectively.

Elkerbout's included studies are self-performed brushing in humans aged 18 and
over, with plaque accumulation from 12 hours to 4 days. Not laboratory models.

## Caries

SIGN 138 §5.7.3: "No evidence was identified to show that powered toothbrushes
are more effective than manual toothbrushes in preventing dental caries **in
children**." That is SIGN's search and SIGN's population. I did not search the
adult primary literature for a caries outcome, and the chapter's absence claim is
bounded accordingly.

## DBOH's own citation

The chapter 13 evidence row for this recommendation cites Wang 2020 and
Elkerbout 2020. DBOH **chapter 8** separately cites the Cochrane review (Yaacob
and colleagues, 2014) for a moderate-certainty plaque and gingivitis statement.
An earlier draft said the guideline had omitted Cochrane; it had not.
