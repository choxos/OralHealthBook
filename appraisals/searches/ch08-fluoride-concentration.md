# Chapter 8: fluoride concentration in toothpaste

**Database:** PubMed
**Date run:** 15 August 2026
**Question:** Does a higher fluoride concentration in toothpaste prevent more
dental caries?

## Deferring to the Cochrane search

The guideline's cited source is a Cochrane review [@walsh2019] whose own search
ran to **15 August 2018** across the Cochrane Oral Health trials register,
CENTRAL, MEDLINE Ovid (1946 onwards) and Embase Ovid (1980 onwards), plus
ClinicalTrials.gov and the WHO ICTRP for ongoing trials, with no language or date
restrictions, and with two people independently selecting studies, extracting
data and assessing risk of bias.

One person with PubMed cannot improve on that, and pretending otherwise would be
the kind of false rigour this book objects to. No independent search was
attempted for the primary studies.

## The gap worth stating

The review includes 96 trials published between **1955 and 2014**. The newest
trial informing the fluoride concentration in your toothpaste is over a decade
old, and the review's authors state that for many of the concentration
comparisons the effect estimates "are uncertain and could be challenged by
further research."

## Correction logged

The original blog post claimed 1,450-1,500ppm "was the only one which led to
lower tooth decay in children and adolescents compared to all the lower doses."
The review reports **one** such head-to-head comparison, against 1,000-1,250ppm,
at SMD -0.08. The blog also claimed amounts over 1,500ppm "didn't perform
better," where the data show no evidence of a difference on intervals that
include worthwhile benefit. Both are corrected in the chapter.
