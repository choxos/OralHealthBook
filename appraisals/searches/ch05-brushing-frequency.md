# Chapter 5: brushing frequency and dental caries

**Database:** PubMed
**Date run:** 15 August 2026
**Question:** Does brushing more often reduce dental caries?

## Query 1

```
toothbrushing frequency dental caries incidence increment systematic review
meta-analysis Journal of Dental Research 2016
```
Date limits 2015 to 2018. **1 record.**

- **27334438** — Kumar S, Tadakamadla J, Johnson NW. Effect of toothbrushing
  frequency on incidence and increment of dental caries: a systematic review and
  meta-analysis. *J Dent Res* 2016;95(11):1230-6.
  doi:10.1177/0022034516655315. **Screened in.** Primary question is exactly the
  question at issue; 33 included studies.

## Query 2

```
(toothbrushing timing OR time of day brushing OR bedtime brushing) AND dental caries
```
No date limits, sorted by relevance, top 20 of **249** reviewed by title.

Kumar 2016 was the top hit. No other systematic review of brushing frequency
with a caries outcome appeared.

## Query 3: is the relevant review cited by the guideline?

Full-text search of all thirteen chapters and the appendix of *Delivering Better
Oral Health* (10 September 2025 edition, archived in `sources/Books/DBOH_UK/`)
for "Kumar".

**Result: not cited.** The name appears in **chapter 6 (oral cancer)** only, as
Kumar N and colleagues on the oral management of oncology patients. A different
author and a different paper. There are no occurrences in chapter 5 (periodontal
diseases); an earlier version of this record said there were.

The only brushing-frequency systematic review DBOH cites anywhere is Zimmermann
and colleagues 2015, on **periodontitis**, and it is cited in **chapter 8 (oral
hygiene)**, in the periodontitis subsection, not in chapter 5. Chapter 8 asserts
"Frequency of brushing is important. Brushing should occur twice daily as a
minimum" with no citation attached to the caries claim.

## Query 4, added after review: the frequency search these queries never ran

The three queries above cannot support the sentence they were used for.

Query 1 is a **known-item** retrieval: it names the journal, the year and the
design, and it is date-limited **2015 to 2018**. It was written to find Kumar,
and it found Kumar. It could not have returned anything published after 2018.

Query 2 is a **timing** search. Its terms are "toothbrushing timing", "time of
day brushing" and "bedtime brushing". A systematic review of brushing
*frequency* would appear in that result set only by accident.

So "nothing more recent came back" described a search that was not looking. Re-run
without the date cap and with frequency terms:

```
toothbrushing frequency AND dental caries AND (systematic review[pt] OR meta-analysis[pt])
```

Screened in, and **not cited anywhere in this book's first draft**:

- **41469640** — Al-Sharani HM, Stormon N, Al-Hutbany N, Zhang Y, Zulfiqar T.
  Optimal tooth brushing initiation age and frequency for preventing early
  childhood caries: a systematic review and meta-analysis. *BMC Oral Health*
  2025;25(1):2006. doi:10.1186/s12903-025-07179-5. Published 30 December 2025.
  32 studies (27 cross-sectional, 5 longitudinal); 8 contributed to the frequency
  meta-analysis. Brushing fewer than twice daily: **OR 2.11**, p = 0.0052, with
  substantial heterogeneity and no evidence of publication bias.

## Conclusion

Kumar 2016 remains the review whose primary question is closest to the
recommendation's, and it is not cited by the guideline. But it is **not** the only
systematic review of brushing frequency and caries published before this book's
search date: Al-Sharani 2025 exists, is limited to early childhood caries and to
observational studies, and points the same way.

The chapter is worded accordingly, and the irony is recorded rather than buried:
a chapter whose complaint is that the guideline failed to cite the relevant review
had itself failed to cite a more recent one, for the same reason, which is that
nobody ran the search that would have found it.

## Limitation

PubMed only, one screener. A CENTRAL and Embase search would be needed before
claiming this is exhaustive, and no such claim is made.
