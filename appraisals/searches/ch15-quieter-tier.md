# Chapter 15: supervised brushing, sugar-free medicines, bedtime sugar

::: provenance
**How this record was produced.** The searches below were run on the dates
stated and disclosed in the chapter at the time. This file was written up
afterwards, from that disclosure and from the working notes, when an audit found
that the chapter pointed at a record file that did not yet exist. It is therefore
a faithful account of what was done, not a contemporaneous log, and it is marked
as such because the alternative is to let a reader assume otherwise. Where a
search failed and had to be re-run, the failure is recorded first.
:::

**Database:** PubMed
**Date run:** 21 August 2026

## Supervised toothbrushing

Checked for an update to dos Santos APP and colleagues' systematic review of
supervised toothbrushing programmes. None identified. Full text held.

## Sugars at bedtime

Checked for an update to Baghlaf K, Muirhead V, Moynihan P, Weston-Price S,
Pine C. Free sugars consumption around bedtime and dental caries in children: a
systematic review. None identified. Full text and supplement held.

The review's own statement on pooling, quoted in full because an earlier draft of
the chapter truncated it inside quotation marks: "It was not possible to conduct
a meta-analysis, because of the considerable variations **in the type of bedtime
exposure and outcome measures**."

## Sugar-free medicines

**No systematic review of sugar-free medicines and dental caries was identified
by this search.** This is a genuine null and it is the weakest-bounded claim in
the chapter: a single PubMed pass by one screener supports "I did not find one,"
and the chapter is worded that way.

## Recommendation wording

The quoted sentence "Use sugar-free versions of medicines if possible" appears in
chapter 2 tables **1a and 1b only**. Tables 1c, 1e and 1g carry differently
worded professional-intervention rows; 1d and 1f have no medicines row. An
earlier draft attributed the sentence to "tables 1a to 1g".
