**Question:** What is the current evidence on the diagnostic accuracy of
adjunctive tests for oral cancer and potentially malignant disorders?

## Pass 1, and why it failed

A general search for systematic reviews of adjunctive oral cancer tests, which
did not surface the Cochrane update. I recorded in the chapter that I had not
obtained the review DBOH cites and was relying on the guideline's account of it.

That was a search failure, not an access problem. The update is freely available
in PubMed Central.

Screened in:

- **34282854** Walsh T, Macey R, Kerr AR, Lingen MW, Ogden GR, Warnakulasuriya S.
  *Cochrane Database Syst Rev* 2021;7(7):CD010276.
  doi:10.1002/14651858.CD010276.pub3. PMCID PMC8407012.
  **Current version.** Own search to 20 October 2020. 63 studies, 79 datasets,
  7,942 lesions.

DBOH cites the 2015 version (CD010276.pub2). The update predates DBOH's
September 2025 edition by more than four years.

## What the update changes

Summary estimates, with certainty per test rather than as a block:

| Test | Sensitivity | Specificity | Certainty (sens / spec) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Oral cytology | 0.90 (0.82-0.94) | 0.94 (0.88-0.97) | moderate / moderate |
| Vital staining | 0.86 (0.79-0.90) | 0.68 (0.58-0.77) | low / very low |
| Light-based, spectroscopy | 0.87 (0.78-0.93) | 0.50 (0.32-0.68) | low / very low |
| Combined | 0.78 (0.45-0.94) | 0.71 (0.53-0.84) | very low / very low |

Authors' conclusion: none can be recommended "as a **replacement** for the
currently used standard of a surgical biopsy and histological assessment," with
cytology offering "the most potential."

Applicability: 33 studies at low concern, generalisable to patients attending
**secondary** care.
