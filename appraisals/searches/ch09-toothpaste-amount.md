**Database:** PubMed
**Date run:** 21 August 2026, in two passes
**Question:** Does the quantity of toothpaste used affect caries, and what is the
current evidence on topical fluoride as a cause of fluorosis?

## Pass 1, and why it failed

Searched for an update to the Cochrane review of topical fluoride and fluorosis
**by review identifier**:

```
CD007693
```

**Returned nothing useful.** I concluded from this that the 2010 version was
still current, and wrote in the chapter that the evidence "has not been
systematically updated in fifteen years."

That conclusion was wrong. The failure was the method: a Cochrane review's
identifier is not reliably indexed as a searchable term in PubMed, so an ID-only
query is not an update search.

## Pass 2

```
Topical fluoride as a cause of dental fluorosis in children[Title]
```

**3 records.** Screened in:

- **38899538** Wong MCM, Zhang R, Luo BW, Glenny AM, Worthington HV, Lo ECM.
  Topical fluoride as a cause of dental fluorosis in children. *Cochrane Database
  Syst Rev* 2024 Jun 20;6(6):CD007693. doi:10.1002/14651858.CD007693.pub3.
  **This is the current version.** Its own search ran to 28 July 2022; 43 studies
  (3 RCTs, 4 cohort, 10 case-control, 26 cross-sectional).
- 20091645 is the 2010 version, superseded.
- 41839071 is an *American Family Physician* summary, not the review.

## Quantity of toothpaste against caries

No trial randomizing the amount of paste against a caries outcome was found.
Walsh 2019 (chapter 8) varies concentration, not quantity. Recorded as a null
from this search, not as a claim about the literature.

## Conclusion

The guideline cites the 2010 review. The 2024 update exists, was published 15
months before DBOH's September 2025 edition, and changes the relevant numbers.
The chapter has been rewritten against the update.
